**c. In your own words, explain the difference between an exon and an intron**
Exons are the parts of a gene that stay in the mature mRNA and contain the information used to make a protein. In contrast, introns are the non-coding parts between exons that are removed during RNA splicing before the mature mRNA is produced.
**d. Describe whether the introns generally appear longer or shorter than the exons in your gene**

# PART G. Short Reflection
**1. What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene's function?**
UCSC showed me the actual location and structure of the CCR5 gene on chromosome 3. It also showed the exons, introns, and different transcript models, which are not obvious from a simple description of the gene’s function.
**2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**
Knowing the exact genomic location helps identify where a variant is found within the gene. It also allows researchers to compare the variant with gene annotations and determine whether it may affect the coding region or another important part of the gene.
**3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**
A variant’s location alone cannot prove that it causes disease. Functional studies and clinical evidence are still needed to understand its actual effect on the gene and the protein.
**4. What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?**
